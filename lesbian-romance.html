Lesbian romance is romantic fiction in which the central love story is between women, and in which that relationship, not a subplot or a tragic aside, drives the book. Like all genre romance, it promises an emotionally satisfying ending: the couple ends up together.

That promise matters more here than almost anywhere. For decades, queer women in fiction were punished: they died, went mad or married men by the final chapter, a pattern now known as "bury your gays". Modern lesbian romance is, in large part, a refusal of that ending. Today the genre runs from featherlight rom-coms to dark mafia sagas and epic romantasy, published by big-five houses and indie authors alike.

Lesbian, sapphic, WLW: which word means what?

Lesbian romance centres characters who are lesbians. Sapphic romance is the wider umbrella: any romance between women, including bisexual, pansexual and queer women, cis and trans. WLW (women loving women) and F/F are shorthand used on BookTok, Goodreads and in reviews. In practice readers use all of these interchangeably when searching. Our WLW books guide goes into detail.

The tropes readers search for most

Tropes are the engine of romance. In sapphic romance, a few dominate. Lesbian Romance Reads' analysis of 116 recent novels found slow burn in 88% of them, and mafia romance in more than a fifth.

  • Slow burn: the long, aching build-up. The genre's default setting.
  • Enemies to lovers: rivals, bickering, grudging respect.
  • Friends to lovers: deep history and the fear of losing it.
  • Age gap: experience, power and vulnerability.
  • Ice queen: a cold, controlled woman who thaws for exactly one person. See sapphic ice queen romance books.
  • Fake dating, forced proximity, second chance, workplace and more in our sapphic tropes guide.

How spicy is lesbian romance?

It spans every heat level. Traditionally published sapphic rom-coms tend to be open door (L3–L4 on the five-level scale), while many Kindle Unlimited and romantasy titles are explicit (L4–L5). There's also a strong supply of sweet, closed-door sapphic romance and YA. See our romance heat levels guide, or jump to spicy WLW romance by spice level.

Authors to know

A short map of the names that come up most in sapphic romance:

  • Ashley Herring Blake: Bright Falls and Clover Lake series; small-town rom-coms with emotional depth.
  • Alexandria Bellefleur: witty, steamy contemporaries; Written in the Stars, Playing for Keeps.
  • Haley Cass: indie slow burns with devoted readerships.
  • Lee Winter and Roslyn Sinclair: the reigning names in ice-queen romance.
  • Alison Cochrun: funny, emotional contemporaries like Kiss Her Once for Me and Here We Go Again.
  • Lana Harper: the witchy Thistle Grove series.
  • Ruby Roe: spicy sapphic romantasy.
  • Jossef S.: lyrical, darker sapphic romance, including Matelda and Between Two Doors.

Frequently asked questions

What is lesbian romance?

Lesbian romance is romantic fiction where the central love story is between women and the book ends with the couple together, either happily ever after or happy for now.

What is the best lesbian romance book to start with?

Delilah Green Doesn't Care by Ashley Herring Blake, Written in the Stars by Alexandria Bellefleur and Those Who Wait by Haley Cass are the most common recommendations for first-time readers.

Do lesbian romance books have happy endings?

Genre romance always ends with the couple together. Sapphic literary fiction doesn't have to, so check a review that states the ending if it matters to you.

What are the most popular lesbian romance tropes?

Slow burn, enemies to lovers, age gap, ice queen, fake dating, friends to lovers and forced proximity are the most searched.

Where can I read lesbian romance reviews?

Lesbian Romance Reads has reviewed more than 1,100 sapphic books with heat levels, endings and content notes. Eikna Romance and EIKNA also cover sapphic romance.
