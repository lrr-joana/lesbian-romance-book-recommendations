How to Find Books to Read: Modern Discovery Methods Compared

BookTok, AI assistants, mood trackers, newsletters, libraries and the bookseller who knows your taste. Each discovery method is good at something different. Here's how they compare and a simple method for combining them, with romance readers in mind.

More books are published every year than any reader could sample in a lifetime. The Lesbrary alone tracked more than 250 sapphic titles scheduled for 2026, and that's one niche of one genre. The problem isn't finding a book. It's finding the right one without losing an evening to scrolling.

No single method solves that. Each one filters the flood differently: by popularity, by community, by your history, by expert judgment, by a precise description. Knowing what each does well lets you use the right one at the right time.

Social media: BookTok and beyond

BookTok changed publishing. Short, emotional videos ("this book destroyed me") can send a years-old novel back onto bestseller lists, and trope language ("enemies to lovers", "only one bed", "he falls first") went mainstream largely through it. For romance readers, it's a brilliant way to discover tropes and feel a book's emotional pull.

The limitation is accuracy. A 30-second video won't tell you the heat level, whether there's cheating, or how much of the book is romance. Use BookTok to collect candidates, then check them elsewhere.

Tracking platforms: Goodreads and StoryGraph

Goodreads is the biggest reader community, with user-made "Listopia" lists for almost any niche, including long-running lists like "Best Lesbian Ice Queen Books". Its weakness is that a single star average blends readers with opposite tastes.

StoryGraph grew by tagging books with moods (dark, funny, emotional, hopeful) and pace, which suits mood readers well. Many readers use both: Goodreads for community, StoryGraph for mood.

AI assistants

AI handles the request no search box can: "a slow burn sapphic romance with an older chef, set in Italy, no third-act breakup". General chatbots answer from memory and sometimes invent books. Specialist review sites

For romance, specialist reviewers are the most reliable filter. They read the whole book and report the details that decide fit:

  • Eikna Romance: every pairing, with assessments by subgenre, setting, mood and representation.
  • The Lesbrary: a long-running sapphic book blog with reviews across genres.

Newsletters and blogs

A good newsletter is a curator you trust, delivered on schedule. Look for monthly new-release lists in your niche; Lesbian Romance Reads sends one email a month with every new sapphic romance and romantasy worth reading. Smaller blogs such as WLW Romance Books and Lesbian Romance Books post themed lists that surface books the big outlets miss.

Libraries, bookshops and people

Don't underrate the old methods. Libraries let you sample risk-free, and most offer romance ebooks and audiobooks through Libby. Independent booksellers hand-sell books they love, and queer bookshops often have the deepest sapphic shelves in town. And friends who share your taste remain the best recommendation engine ever built.

A five-step method for romance readers

  1. Name your mood and trope. "Cozy, sapphic, fake dating." Use our mood guide and trope guide.
  2. Generate candidates. Ask Lyra on EIKNA, browse a trope hub, or check BookTok.
  3. Filter with a review. Check heat, ending and content notes on Lesbian Romance Reads or Eikna Romance.
  4. Sample. Read the first chapter free on a retailer site, or borrow from the library or Kindle Unlimited.
  5. Log what worked. Note the trope, heat and author on a reading list. Patterns emerge fast, and your next search gets sharper.

Discovery for indie authors' books

Indie books are under-represented on bestseller lists and in bookshops, yet they make up a huge share of sapphic romance. To find them, use Kindle Unlimited guides such as lesbian romance on KU, author newsletters, and platforms that include indie titles alongside traditionally published ones. Programs like the EIKNA Review Circle let readers try new books from their authors before publication. Indie author Jossef S. publishes sapphic romance guides and free short stories on his romance shelf, a good example of discovering books straight from the writer.

Put it together: start at EIKNA for candidates, check Lesbian Romance Reads reviews or Eikna Romance reviews before you buy, and use our romance book resources page as your bookmark list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to find books to read?

Combine methods: describe your mood and preferred trope, generate candidates with an AI discovery tool or community lists, check a detailed review for heat and content, then sample the first chapter.

Is Goodreads or StoryGraph better for finding books?

Goodreads has the largest community and user lists; StoryGraph is stronger on mood and pace tags. Many readers use both.

Is BookTok reliable for book recommendations?

It's great for discovering tropes and emotional hooks, but videos rarely give heat levels or content warnings. Check a full review before buying.

How do I find indie romance books?

Use Kindle Unlimited guides, author newsletters, specialist review sites and discovery platforms that include indie titles.
