Romance Heat Levels Explained: Clean, Closed Door, Open Door and Spicy

"Spice level" is one of the first things romance readers ask about, and one of the worst-labelled. Here's a plain-language scale, what each level actually contains, and how to check a book's heat before you start.

Heat level describes how much sexual content is on the page in a romance novel. It has nothing to do with how good, serious or romantic a book is. A clean romance can be devastating; an explicit one can be fluffy. Heat only answers one question: when the characters get intimate, does the camera stay in the room?

Publishers rarely print heat levels on covers, and marketing language ("steamy", "sizzling", "swoony") is inconsistent. That's why readers built their own systems, from chili peppers on BookTok to numbered scales on review sites.

The five-level heat scale

We use the same five-level scale as Lesbian Romance Reads, which rates every one of its 1,100+ reviews this way. It's simple enough to remember and precise enough to be useful.

LevelCommon namesWhat's on the page
L1Sweet, clean, kisses onlyAttraction, kissing, maybe a shared bed with nothing more. No sex scenes.
L2Closed door, fade to blackSex happens but the scene cuts away before it starts or resumes afterwards.
L3Open door, steamySex scenes are on the page, written with some detail but not graphic language. Usually one to three scenes.
L4Explicit, spicyDetailed, explicit scenes with frank language. Intimacy is a meaningful part of the story.
L5Extra spicy, scorchingFrequent, central sex scenes; the relationship often develops through them. May include kink.

Chili-pepper ratings usually map roughly as one pepper = L1–L2, two to three peppers = L3, four peppers = L4 and five peppers = L5, though every reviewer draws the lines slightly differently.

Clean and sweet romance (L1)

Clean romance is a booming category, not a niche. Readers choose it for many reasons: personal or religious values, reading in public or aloud, or simply because they're here for the yearning rather than the sex. Inspirational (faith-based) romance sits here, as do many cozy rom-coms and a lot of YA.

A tip: "clean" sometimes also implies no swearing or no alcohol, especially in Christian romance. If you just want no sex scenes but don't mind language, search for "sweet romance" or "closed door" instead.

Closed door romance (L2)

Closed door means the relationship is physical, and the book makes that clear, but the reader isn't shown the scene. Many mainstream rom-coms are closed or "cracked" door. It's a good fit for readers who want an adult relationship without explicit content.

Sapphic readers looking for this level should try the feel-good lesbian romance list, which flags lower-heat picks.

Open door and steamy romance (L3)

L3 is where most traditionally published contemporary romance sits. Scenes are on the page and matter to the characters' arc, but the writing leans emotional over anatomical. Many of Ashley Herring Blake's and Alexandria Bellefleur's sapphic rom-coms fall here or edge into L4.

Explicit and spicy romance (L4–L5)

Spicy romance is huge on Kindle Unlimited and in romantasy. At L4 and L5, intimacy scenes are long, frequent and explicit, and sometimes the plot is built around them. Erotic romance (L5) still requires a central love story and a happy ending; erotica does not.

Heat is not darkness. A book can be L5 and sweet, or L2 and very dark. Dark romance refers to themes such as violence, crime, captivity or dubious consent, which need their own content warnings. See Eikna Romance's content warnings guide.

How to check a book's heat before you read

  1. Check a heat-rated review. Lesbian Romance Reads lists heat on every review; Eikna Romance and EIKNA show heat alongside tropes.
  2. Read the first sex scene placement. Many readers skim reviews for "first kiss at 60%" type notes. Slow burns often hold heat until late.
  3. Look at the publisher and format. Some imprints and Kindle Unlimited series lean steamy; others specialise in sweet romance. The book's own description often signals it with words like "steamy" or "sweet".
  4. Search with the word "spice". On EIKNA you can describe what you want in plain language, for example "sapphic slow burn, closed door, happy ending", and the Lyra assistant filters from real book data.
  5. Remember it's personal. One reader's "medium" is another's "a lot". Use the scale as a guide, not a guarantee.

Heat levels and the HEA

Heat and ending are separate questions, and both matter. If a guaranteed happy ending is non-negotiable, check the HEA and HFN guide, or go straight to lesbian books with happy endings.

Frequently asked questions

What does closed door romance mean?

Closed door romance includes a sexual relationship but cuts away before intimate scenes, so the reader isn't shown them. It sits at level 2 on a five-level heat scale.

What is a spicy book?

A spicy book contains explicit sex scenes on the page. Readers often rate spice with chili peppers or a numbered scale; spicy usually means level 4 or 5.

Is clean romance the same as Christian romance?

Not always. Clean romance simply has no sex scenes. Christian or inspirational romance is usually clean but also includes faith themes and often avoids swearing.

Are sapphic romance books usually spicy?

Sapphic romance spans every heat level. Traditionally published sapphic rom-coms tend to sit at L3–L4, while Kindle Unlimited and romantasy titles are often L4–L5. Lesbian Romance Reads lists the heat of every book it reviews.

Does a high heat level mean a book is dark?

No. Heat measures sexual content; darkness measures themes like violence or coercion. A book can be very explicit and very sweet.
