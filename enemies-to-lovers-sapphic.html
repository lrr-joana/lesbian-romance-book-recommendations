Enemies to Lovers Sapphic Romance

Two women who can't stand each other, a reason they can't walk away, and banter sharp enough to draw blood. Here's why enemies to lovers works so well in sapphic romance, and the lesbian rivals-to-lovers books that do it best.

Enemies to lovers is the trope where the leads start in conflict, through rivalry, opposing goals, an old grudge or genuine dislike, and end in love. Its appeal is the transformation: insults turning into flirting, contempt turning into respect, the moment one of them realises she'd rather lose the fight than lose the other woman.

In sapphic romance, the trope often trades physical menace for intellectual sparring. These are women who are evenly matched, who see through each other, and who are furious about it.

Enemies vs rivals: know what you're getting

  • True enemies: genuine opposition. Opposite sides of a war, a feud, a betrayal. Higher angst, more to repair.
  • Rivals: competitors who respect each other underneath. Workplace rivals, academic rivals, sports rivals. Lower angst, more banter.
  • Frenemies or former friends: a broken friendship to mend. Often the most emotional version.

Fantasy enemies to lovers

Fantasy gives the trope its highest stakes: warring kingdoms, opposing magics, assassins and their targets.

  • The Jasmine Throne by Tasha Suri: an imprisoned princess and a maidservant with hidden power, pulled together by mutual need and mistrust.
  • Dreamslayer by A.K. Mulford (December 1, 2026): an acolyte trained to destroy nightmares falls for one, Reverie, her sworn enemy.
  • The Witch and the Huntress by Luna McNamara (2026): Medea and Atalanta on opposite ends of Jason's quest.

Ice queen enemies

A huge share of sapphic enemies to lovers involves an ice queen: the cold boss, the rival CEO, the editor who shreds everyone's work. The tension comes from one woman refusing to be intimidated by the other. Lee Winter's The Brutal Truth and Jae's Something in the Wine are frequently recommended. Dark enemies to lovers

In dark romance, "enemies" can mean opposite sides of a crime war, a captor and captive, or a woman sent to destroy the person she ends up loving. Expect morally grey heroines and content warnings. What makes enemies to lovers work

  1. A real reason for the conflict. Not a misunderstanding a single conversation would fix.
  2. An equal match. Both women need to be capable of winning.
  3. Forced proximity. A shared project, cabin, quest or car. Without it, enemies just avoid each other.
  4. Earned respect before attraction. The turn should come from seeing who the other woman really is.
  5. Accountability. If one of them was cruel, the book should deal with it. Grovelling is part of the genre's contract.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best enemies to lovers sapphic book?

Popular picks include Here We Go Again by Alison Cochrun, Payback's a Witch by Lana Harper and Playing for Keeps by Alexandria Bellefleur. For fantasy, try The Jasmine Throne by Tasha Suri.

What is the difference between enemies to lovers and rivals to lovers?

Enemies genuinely oppose or dislike each other at the start; rivals compete but usually respect each other. Rivals stories tend to be lighter.

Are there sapphic enemies to lovers books on Kindle Unlimited?

Yes. Lesbian Romance Reads keeps a checked list of enemies to lovers lesbian romance on Kindle Unlimited.

Is enemies to lovers usually a slow burn?

Often, because the characters need time to move from hostility to trust. Many books combine both tropes.
